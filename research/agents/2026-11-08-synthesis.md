# Agent research synthesis — 2026-11-08

## Run status

18 independent research slices completed through the runtime `agent()` API. Results indexed at `research/agents/2026-11-08-wave-index.md`. Facebook was handled separately through Agent-Reach; Instagram deferred after an upstream failure.

## Evidence quality rule

Agent memos contain useful leads but are not automatically facts. Some memos give precise numbers, route times, price ranges, or failure rates without a directly verifiable source for each number. This synthesis keeps only claims that are either directly linked to a credible source, repeated by independent evidence, or explicitly labels the claim as a hypothesis to test.

## Strongest convergent findings

### 1. Use preorder/drop as first operating shape

Multiple slices converge on scheduled preorder, capped capacity, and batched delivery. This is supported by the perishable-product preorder literature and by croissant process constraints. It is a recommendation/inference, not proof of demand.

Useful test: one fixed production day, one local/short route, one Hanoi zone, paid orders only.

### 2. Treat texture as a delivery KPI

Operations and packaging slices consistently identify moisture migration, warm packing, crushing, and stacking as the main delivery-quality risks. Puratos directly recommends breathable/partially open paper or perforated packaging, avoiding stacking, and spreading baking through the day. The exact vent size, cooling threshold, and box construction proposed by agents are engineering hypotheses, not validated requirements.

Useful test: compare two box/venting variants over the same route; record arrival photo, crust score, crumb score, and time-to-eat.

### 3. Do not promise exact route ETA before measuring it

Delivery slice evidence supports several plausible corridors and published service bands, but not an exact BAMIDAYNE-to-customer ETA. The agent claim that all inner-Hanoi routes take a fixed 35–50 minutes is not accepted as fact. Measure at morning, midday, and evening windows with the actual courier service.

### 4. Keep menu small while process stabilizes

The product, lamination, climate, and filled-quality slices all point to process variance when fillings and temperature conditions change. The conservative product proposal remains: one plain reference croissant, one familiar sweet, one savory, and one rotating seasonal item. This is strategy, not market fact.

### 5. Separate filling classes

Agent technical findings distinguish bake-stable fillings (for example chocolate, frangipane, bake-stable jam, hard cheese) from post-bake cream/curd-style fillings that change moisture and reheating behavior. Use this as recipe-development guidance, not a final production specification. Every filling needs a bench test.

### 6. Price evidence needs normalization

The price agent collected useful leads, but several exact values are not linked line-by-line to stable product pages in the memo. Existing direct page evidence remains safer: plain listings around 18,000–20,000 VND and an almond listing at 36,000 VND were observed earlier, but size/date/channel are not fully normalized. Do not set final price from agent ranges until a fresh menu sheet records URL, date, weight, filling, and delivery conditions.

### 7. Social evidence is discovery-only so far

Facebook search returned pages/groups related to croissant after login, but profile text and engagement metrics were sparse. Instagram search failed upstream. No social conversion or demand conclusion is justified.

## Findings treated as hypotheses only

- Local premium breakfast willingness to pay.
- Exact population and density figures for Phu Lo.
- Exact 20–30 km distance and 35–60 minute route time to every Hanoi zone.
- Any claim that a premium single croissant cannot sell locally.
- Any “100% preorder,” “zero waste,” “zero walk-in,” or “unviable” claim.
- Any exact freshness window such as 15–20 minutes, 4–8 hours, or 24 hours without a controlled test on the actual recipe and packaging.
- Any exact 2-day/48–72-hour schedule until the baker’s recipe, freezer/chiller capacity, and staff schedule are known.
- Any exact customer segment conversion rate.
